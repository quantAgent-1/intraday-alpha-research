## DR-X10 — Modality A (academic / web) findings

*Lane: buyback intraday execution (gates hypothesis F2 — 10b-18-fenced buyback POV algos providing intraday
support on soft mornings in high-dosage names like GOOGL). Modality A = peer-reviewed / SSRN / working-paper
literature via web search. Refuter default honored: any claim I could not source to a study is marked UNVERIFIED,
not asserted.*

### Verdict recommendation

**OPEN-TESTABLE (confidence MED-LOW).** The *payer and the mechanism* F2 rests on are well documented: actual
US open-market repurchases (i) provide price support / provide liquidity when other investors sell, and (ii) are
price-conditioned — firms buy *after* declines. That much is CONFIRMED at daily/monthly frequency by multiple
top-journal papers with real repurchase data. **But the specific object F2 needs — an *intraday conditional
asymmetry* (present-into-weakness, passive-into-strength within the session) that is *measurably* different in
*high-dosage* names — is UNVERIFIED: no study confirms it and, critically, no study refutes it either, because
US intraday/daily repurchase execution data is private (the SEC rule that would have created it was VACATED in
Dec 2023).** The one same-day magnitude in the literature (UK, +5–29 bps) is a *disclosure-event* effect that
does not transport to the post-vacatur US. One documented intraday pattern is an active headwind for the GOOGL
cell: Nasdaq repurchasers concentrate execution at *end-of-day*, not across the mid-session window F2 targets.
So the wave's kill bar ("a competent intraday study showing zero conditional footprint on megacaps ⇒ F2 dies")
is NOT met — but neither is the confirming bar. The literature makes F2 a *reasonable* $0 owned-data screen,
not a pre-validated edge.

**What would flip it:** a study with *actual intraday US repurchase-execution timestamps* (or a clean
identification off the vacated-then-revived disclosure) showing either (a) a measurable within-session
present-into-weakness footprint that scales with program dosage → flips toward CONFIRMED/OPEN-STRONG, or
(b) zero conditional intraday footprint on large-cap names once you control for generic buy-the-dip → flips to
EXHAUSTED-BY-FIELD. Neither exists today.

### Mechanism

Who pays: the corporation. An announced $-program is executed by a broker POV algo under Rule 10b-18's safe
harbor, whose **price condition** caps each purchase at ≤ max(highest independent bid, last independent
transaction) — so the algo *structurally cannot lead the market up*; it goes passive on upticks and its fills
cluster into flat/weak tape. The safe harbor also **excludes the opening purchase and (for high-volume issuers,
which the megacaps are) the last 10 minutes**, fencing this flow OUT of the auctions and INTO the continuous
session — exactly F2's window. Price-insensitivity within the day is real: the mandate is completion of an
authorized $-amount, often via mechanical 10b5-1 grids that keep executing through blackouts. Persistence:
programs run for years. This is a coherent, named, price-insensitive, publicly-dosed payer — it clears the
"is there a payer at all" bar that most dead intraday families failed. **The open question the literature does
NOT settle is whether the price-condition binds often enough on a 1-tick megacap book to create a *tradable*
conditional asymmetry, versus merely impounding a symmetric level of support.**

### Claims

C1 [CONFIRMED] (T2, RFS 2004, sample ~1993–94, 64 firms w/ proprietary trade data): NYSE repurchasers are
price-sensitive and **buy back after stock-price declines**; intraday, NYSE firms show a **U-shaped**
repurchase pattern while **Nasdaq firms concentrate repurchases at end-of-day** — the price-conditioning F2
assumes is real, but the intraday-timing evidence is a headwind for a *Nasdaq* name (GOOGL) traded in the
*mid-session* window. Cook, Krigman & Leach, "On the Timing and Execution of Open Market Repurchases."
https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/17/2/463/1576952

C2 [CONFIRMED] (T2, JFE 2016, US actual repurchases, 50,204 repurchase-months 2004–2010, IV-causal): repurchases
**improve liquidity and provide liquidity when other investors sell the stock or in times of crisis** — the
"support-into-weakness" mechanism, but established at *monthly* frequency, not intraday. Hillert, Maug &
Obernberger, "Stock Repurchases and Liquidity." https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X15001488

C3 [CONFIRMED] (T2, RFS 2017, US actual repurchases, manually collected): repurchases make prices more
efficient and **the effects are primarily driven by repurchases in DOWN markets — firms provide price support
at fundamental values**. Confirms state-dependence of the flow but at daily/market-state level, no intraday
identification. Busch & Obernberger, "Actual Share Repurchases, Price Efficiency, and the Information Content
of Stock Prices." https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/30/1/324/2669974

C4 [CONFIRMED] (T2, JBFA 2006, 60,000+ TSX repurchase trades): repurchase trades carry a **negative intraday
price impact**, but the price decline following them is **less pronounced than for matched non-repurchase
trades** (a stabilization/support signature), with abnormal losses *before* and gains *after* the trade
(timing). Direct intraday support evidence — but Canadian market, older structure, no bps figure extractable.
McNally, Smith & Barnes, "The Price Impacts of Open Market Repurchase Trades."
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228315600_The_Price_Impact_of_Open_Market_Share_Repurchases

C5 [PLAUSIBLE, disclosure-driven, NOT US-transportable] (T2, SSRN/EFMA 2025 working paper, UK daily buyback
disclosures): daily-disclosed buybacks generate an **immediate same-day return of +0.05% to +0.29% (5–29 bps)**
and **narrow bid-ask spreads by 0.20–0.50%**; a trading strategy on the daily disclosures yields ~7–50%
annualized. Managers buy after declines. CRITICAL CAVEAT the paper itself makes: **it is the *disclosure* that
drives the return, not the size of the buy** — this is a next-day-mandatory-disclosure event effect, and the
US has NO such disclosure (see C6), so this magnitude does NOT carry to F2's US no-disclosure setting.
Andriosopoulos, "Does the daily reporting of share buybacks matter?"
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5287917

C6 [CONFIRMED — data blocker] (T1, US Court of Appeals 5th Cir., 19 Dec 2023): the SEC's Share Repurchase
Disclosure Modernization Rule — which would have required firms to collect **daily** repurchase data and file it
quarterly — was **VACATED** (Chamber of Commerce v. SEC; arbitrary-and-capricious / APA). Consequence for F2:
**there is no public US daily or intraday repurchase-execution series**; F2 cannot use disclosure-based
identification and must infer dosage from quarterly 10-Q aggregates ÷ trading days (crude, as F2 concedes) and
infer *presence* purely from owned quote/price behavior. https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/fifth-circuit-vacates-sec-share-repurchase-disclosure-rule

C7 [CONFIRMED] (T2, RFS 2014, US monthly actual-repurchase disclosures): firms **buy back at a low price
relative to the average market price of the month** (bargain-price timing), positive abnormal returns
concentrated around disclosure. Confirms managerial timing skill — but monthly, and the return is around
*disclosure*, not an intraday execution footprint an outsider could ride. Ben-Rephael, Oded & Wohl, "Do Firms
Buy Their Stock at Bargain Prices?" https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1738922

C8 [UNVERIFIED — the F2 trade class is neither demonstrated nor refuted intraday] (T3, practitioner, Elm Wealth
n.d.): buyback *predictability* "invites some front-running by astute trading firms and hedge funds" who
"likely" earn attractive risk-adjusted returns, eroded but not eliminated by competition — explicitly labeled
*speculative, no empirical estimate*. Sizing color: ~$6bn/day US buybacks ≈ **0.5% of daily equity volume**,
Grinold-Kahn implied impact ≈ 0.07%/day, which the authors call "too high" pending decay adjustment. No study
demonstrates the specific F2 trade (buy soft mornings in buyback-heavy US names) succeeding OR failing.
https://elmwealth.com/stock-buybacks/

C9 [PLAUSIBLE — dosage cross-section, but about firm CHOICE not intraday behavior] (T2, JFQA 2024): repurchase
*intensity* is cross-sectionally endogenous to price-impact considerations — "fragile" (higher-liquidity) stocks
repurchase less because price impact is smaller; causally identified via asset-manager mergers. This is the
closest thing to a dosage cross-section, but it explains *how much firms choose to buy*, not *whether high-dose
names behave differently intraday*. No intraday dosage study located. "Repurchases for Price Impact: Evidence
from Fragile Stocks." https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A186D7CAF532BD1F9830444C29488FCB

C10 [CONFIRMED — orthogonal horizon warning] (T2, JFE 1995 / 2000 + 2009): the documented, out-of-sample-durable
buyback edge is **long-horizon drift** — ~+12.1% four-year BHAR after announcement (up to +45% for value
names), ~7%/yr over 3 years — *not* an intraday execution effect. The field's actual "buyback anomaly" lives at
the multi-year announcement horizon and is orthogonal to F2; F2 must not be confused with, or credited by, this
literature. Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen; Peyer & Vermaelen "Nature and Persistence of Buyback Anomalies."
https://www.efmaefm.org/0efmameetings/efma%20annual%20meetings/2006-Madrid/papers/537087_full.pdf

### Economics sketch

Cited magnitudes, normalized: the *transportable* US evidence gives NO clean intraday bps figure — it gives
monthly liquidity provision (C2), down-market price support (C3), and multi-year drift (C10). The only same-day
number is UK **+5–29 bps** (C5), but that is a *disclosure* effect the US structurally lacks (C6), so it is an
*upper-bound analogue*, not a US prior. Practitioner sizing (C8) implies a *symmetric* level impact ≈ 7 bps/day
on ~0.5% participation — of which F2 seeks only the *state-dependent redistribution* onto soft afternoons,
plausibly a fraction. F2's own honest prior (+4–8 bps gross, modal outcome BETWEEN or NULL) is consistent with
this literature and I do not raise it. Our cost burden for a GOOGL continuous round-trip ≈ 2.5–3.3 bps ×2 floor
≈ **5–6.5 bps**. Net prior therefore straddles zero: a +6 bps gross effect nets ~0–3 bps — *below* the
champion. **Comparison line: champion = +2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 bps/event holdout (single-print auction,
survivor 5/5); F2 = +4–8 bps gross prior, ~5–6.5 bps cost, net prior ≈ 0–3 bps, survivor 3/5.** F2 is a
diagnostic-grade screen, not a champion-class candidate — its value is a permanent map entry on the buyback
axis, not an expected deploy.

### Proposed next test (OPEN-TESTABLE)

- **Hypothesis:** On a name's soft-morning day (09:35→11:30 return in own TRAIN bottom tercile), the afternoon
  (11:30→15:30) mean return is positive and **monotone in buyback dosage** (GOOGL > tier-2 semis > placebo
  TSLA/MU), reflecting present-into-weakness 10b-18 execution.
- **Named payer:** corporate 10b-18 buyback POV algo (price-capped, completion-mandated, ex-open/ex-last-10-min).
- **Data:** owned GOOGL + 9-name 1s quotes (2020→2026); dosage from $0 SEC XBRL quarterly repurchase series
  (M21 tooling, PIT-lagged to filing dates per landmine L5). No purchase.
- **Universe:** GOOGL cell (C1); dose-ladder all 10 (C2); GOOGL-minus-placebo diff (C3, the mechanism-isolating
  kill prong).
- **Expected n & power:** GOOGL ≈ 513 soft-morning events; σ(11:30→15:30)≈90 bps → se≈4 bps → detects ≥8 bps.
  Dose-ladder = sign/ordering test (se on tier-diff ≈6 bps), powered as ordinal not levels — pre-declare.
- **A-priori thresholds:** screen PASS = pooled mid-alpha ≥ ~6 bps (at cost floor) AND day-clustered CI-lo > 0
  AND **C3 GOOGL-minus-placebo CI-lo > 0** (separates buyback support from dead generic buy-the-dip — C10/dip
  literature is the control, not the thesis). Economics only on screen PASS (M20/M27 ordering).
- **Kill criteria:** C3 fails (placebo names show equal lift) ⇒ mechanism dead regardless of C1; OR pooled
  screen BETWEEN ⇒ close with a conditioner note (admissible long-side feature), no revival promise; OR the
  Nasdaq-end-of-day execution pattern (C1) manifests as the afternoon-11:30→15:30 window carrying *no* footprint
  while a 15:30→15:50 window would (retest that boundary once, pre-declared, before closing).
- **Trial family charged:** new `buyback_dosage_afternoon_v1` family; declares adjacency debt to M18/DR-X7
  (reversion) and M19 (flow-conditioned reversion), differentiated by named non-close intraday payer + C3 kill.

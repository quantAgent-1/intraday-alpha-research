## DR-X9 — Modality A (academic literature) findings

### Verdict recommendation

**EXHAUSTED-BY-FIELD (confidence MED-HIGH)** for the specific thesis M20 Stage-2 needs — that the *initial price reaction CONTINUES at minutes-to-hours horizons in US large-cap cash equities* after scheduled macro releases. The peer-reviewed intraday decomposition literature is unusually consistent and points the *opposite* way: the macro-announcement equity premium is realized almost entirely in the *pre*-announcement window (overnight / up to ~5 min before the release), and *post*-release index returns are on average small and statistically insignificant (with large variance) across FOMC, NFP, ISM and GDP. Three independent T2 sources (Lucca–Moench 2015; Hu–Pan–Wang–Zhu 2021; Ernst–Gilbert–Hrdlicka 2019/2022) converge on "flat after the release." On top of that, even the *pre*-release drift — the piece that DID carry returns — has a documented post-2015 OOS decay (Kurov–Wolfe–Gilbert 2021, a McLean–Pontiff-class kill). So the drift is (a) in the wrong window for a post-release reaction trade and (b) decaying where it existed. This clears the pre-commitment bar in WAVE5_PLAN: ≥2 unrefuted OOS/intraday-decomposition results covering our release classes say there is no unconditional post-release continuation to harvest.

**What would flip it:** a primary intraday study (or our own Stage-1 screen) showing a *statistically significant, post-2015, post-release* (post-2:00pm for FOMC; post-open for 8:30/10:00 releases) equity continuation in cash large-caps that is *directional/conditional on the surprise* — not the unconditional average, which the literature has settled as ≈0. The academic gap is precisely here: essentially all decompositions test the *unconditional* pre/post split; a *surprise-signed* post-release continuation at minutes–hours in single names is under-tested and is the only crack left. That is a narrow OPEN-CONDITIONAL, not a green light.

### Mechanism

The literature's payer is **not** a slow post-release re-leverer; it is an **ex-ante risk premium for the resolution of aggregate uncertainty**, paid *into* the announcement, not out of it. Savor–Wilson / Ai–Bansal frame it as compensation for bearing systematic announcement-day risk; Hu–Pan–Wang–Zhu (2021) add a second "impact-uncertainty" risk that *builds up and then resolves before the clock hits the release*, generating a pre-announcement return with *no* abnormal variance and *no* post-release drift. Under that mechanism there is no coherent slow payer *after* the print: the risk is resolved at the print, variance spikes, and the mean move is a coin-flip. The one candidate slow-payer mechanism the academic corpus does NOT rule out — vol-target / risk-parity re-levering latency after the variance spike — is not measured in this (academic) modality; it is the explicit charge of Modality C. Academic verdict on the payer: for post-release *continuation*, **no coherent payer found** in the asset-pricing literature; the documented flow is a pre-release premium, which a post-release reaction window cannot capture.

### Claims

C1 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2021 *Finance Research Letters*-forthcoming / SSRN 2021, sample Sep-1994–May-2018): **Post-announcement equity index returns are on average small and statistically insignificant, while pre-announcement returns carry the premium** — pre-announcement returns average NFP 10.1 bps, ISM 9.1 bps, GDP 7.5 bps (vs 0.69 bps on non-announcement days), realized from the prior close to ~5 min before the release; "post-announcement, the average returns for NFP, ISM, and GDP are small and insignificant, while exhibiting large variances, similar to the post-announcement patterns for FOMC." FOMC pre-announcement return = 27.1 bps in their sample. — Hu, Pan, Wang, Zhu, "Premium for Heightened Uncertainty," https://en.saif.sjtu.edu.cn/junpan/PreAnn_20210302.pdf. *This is the load-bearing kill: it is the direct intraday resolution the WAVE brief asked for, and it says post-release ≈ 0.*

C2 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2015 *Journal of Finance* 70(1); NY Fed Staff Report 512, sample Sep-1994–Mar-2011): **The FOMC equity move is a PRE-announcement drift** — S&P rises ≈49 bps in the 24h before the 2:15pm announcement (≈80% of the annual/equity-premium return over the sample); right after the release stocks fall ~15 min then recover and end near the 2pm level — i.e. *post-2pm continuation is flat*. Gross, NO-COST-MODEL. — Lucca & Moench, "The Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift," https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr512.pdf.

C3 [CONFIRMED] (T2, SSRN 3134546, *Finance Research Letters* 2021, sample extended to Dec-2019): **The pre-FOMC drift decayed OOS post-2015** — mean 24h pre-FOMC return ≈44.5 bps in Apr-2011–Dec-2015 (press-conference meetings) collapsed to ≈9.2 bps and *statistically insignificant* in Jan-2016–Dec-2019; disappearance in non-press-conference meetings too; authors attribute it to reduced uncertainty (VIX). A genuine McLean–Pontiff-class post-publication decay. **Dissent noted:** Ben Dor & Rosa (2019) report *no* change after 2015 — so decay is CONFIRMED-with-one-dissent, not unanimous. — Kurov, Wolfe, Gilbert, https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf.

C4 [CONFIRMED] (T2, *Journal of Financial Economics* 2014 "Asset Pricing: A Tale of Two Days"; NBER w24432, sample 1958–2009): **The announcement premium is a DAY-level, market-BETA effect** — mean market excess return 11.4 bps on announcement days vs 1.1 bps other days (>60% of the equity premium on ~13% of days); CAPM security-market-line is positive/significant *only* on announcement days, across individual stocks and test portfolios. Implication for single-name expression: a megacap's announcement return ≈ its beta × the market's (pre-release) move — there is no separate *idiosyncratic post-release* continuation to harvest per-name. Gross, NO-COST-MODEL, day-level. — Savor & Wilson, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24432/w24432.pdf.

C5 [CONFIRMED] (T2, SSRN 3469703, posted 2019, presented AFA-2022/NFA-2021, sample built on the prior literature's windows): **Adversarial refinement — the announcement premium is smaller than headline and is realized pre-release.** Correcting sample-selection (day-of-month FE, full macro distribution) cuts the "100%+ of the equity premium" claim to *about half*; in their decomposition the announcement-day premium ≈12 bps by 4pm of which ≈9 bps is realized from 4pm prior day to *5 min before* the release. Confirms the pre-release timing and warns the raw premium is inflated. — Ernst, Gilbert, Hrdlicka, "More Than 100% of the Equity Premium…," https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=3469703.

C6 [PLAUSIBLE] (T2, arXiv "Structural Limits of OHLCV-Based Intraday Signals in MNQ Futures," 2026-ish preprint — provenance uncaveated): a systematic falsification on high-impact USD releases (FOMC/CPI/NFP) finds the measurable post-release drift is *entirely contained in the initial bars*; measured from bar 6 onward all horizons give very weak T-stats. Directionally corroborates C1/C2 for futures at minutes scale, but it is a non-peer-reviewed preprint (treat as T2/T3, DECAY of any minutes-drift). — arXiv:2605.04004.

C7 [PLAUSIBLE] (T2, NBER w24748 / BFI WP 2020-39, "Monetary Momentum," Neuhierl & Weber, sample ~1994–2016): a large **DAY-level** FOMC drift — cumulative expansionary-minus-contractionary return ≈2.5% to the decision day, rising to ≈4.5% by +15 days; strategy Sharpe ≈4× buy-and-hold. **Out of the minutes-hours scope** (horizon = days, −25d to +15d) and **DECAY-UNKNOWN** (circulated as a working paper; I found no top-journal publication or clean OOS replication). Listed because the brief named it; it is not a post-release-minutes object. — https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24748/w24748.pdf.

C8 [UNVERIFIED] (T3/T4 for equities): **Treasury auction-result → equity spillover at minutes-hours has no academic documentation I could find.** The academic "Treasury auction" literature is bond-market: an auction *risk premium* (dealers earn ≈40.5 bps auction→settlement; Sigaux, ECB WP 2208 on trading ahead of auctions) and intraday Treasury pricing/liquidity (Fleming). Equity reactions to weak/strong auctions (tails/stop-throughs at ~13:01) are asserted only in practitioner/journalistic sources (Mott Capital, tickeron) — T3/T4, hypothesis-generation only. **UNKNOWN academically; flag as a genuine gap, not a supported edge.** — Sigaux, https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2208.en.pdf; "The Treasury auction risk premium," ScienceDirect S0378426624002309.

C9 [PLAUSIBLE] (T2, cross-section: Chen 2025 *Financial Review* "High-beta stock valuation around macroeconomic announcements"; Ai–Han–Xie "The Cross Section of the Monetary Policy Announcement Premium" 2023): announcement-day cross-sectional dispersion is organized by **beta**, and information asymmetry rises pre-FOMC *only for high-beta stocks*. Reinforces that per-name expression is a beta loading on the (pre-release) market move — supports the power pessimism in the gate table, not a post-release single-name signal. — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fire.12408 ; https://www.hengjieai.com/uploads/1/0/6/6/106628821/announcement_cross_section23.pdf.

### Economics sketch

Expected gross for the *thesis as written* (unconditional post-release minutes–hours continuation, cash large-caps): **≈0 bps** — the direct intraday decompositions (C1, C2) put the post-release mean at "small and insignificant." The returns that DO exist are ≈10–49 bps but sit in the *pre*-release window (C1–C3) and have *decayed* since 2015 where largest (C3). Our cost burden for a continuous-market taker round-trip at $1k–$10k is on the order of spread + impact (single-digit-to-low-double-digit bps), i.e. the structure would pay costs against a ~0 gross. **Net prior: negative-to-zero.** Comparison line: **champion = +2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 bps holdout** at a single-print auction cross with a named indexed-flow payer (survivor 5/5); DR-X9's post-release continuation object is +0 gross, no auction, no payer (survivor ~1/5) — it is not in the same class and does not beat the champion on any gate.

### Proposed next test

Not OPEN-TESTABLE as an *unconditional* continuation study (the field has answered ≈0). The *only* residual, and it is a narrow OPEN-CONDITIONAL for the M20 Stage-1 screen to falsify cheaply on owned data: **H0 — post-release (post-2:00pm FOMC; post-open for 8:30/10:00 releases) 5-min-to-2-h cash-equity returns in {NVDA,TSLA,AMD,MU,GOOGL}, *signed by the release surprise direction*, show no continuation beyond bar 5.** Named (would-be) payer: vol-target/risk-parity re-levering latency after the variance spike (unproven here — hand to Modality C to confirm/deny before any Stage-2). Data: owned XNAS 1s TOB 2020–2026 (no purchase). Universe: 5 names + SPY/QQQ anchor. Power: ~low-hundreds events; expect to *fail to reject H0* given C1 — treat a rejection as the surprise that would flip the verdict. A-priori threshold: require post-bar-5 signed continuation ≥ +10 bps/event with t≥3 pooled AND surviving the post-2015 subsample (guards against the C3 decay). Promotion rule: only a post-2015-robust, surprise-conditional result promotes to Stage-2; the unconditional mean does not. Kill: any horizon whose signal is confined to bars 0–5 (execution-inaccessible at 25 s manual latency) is dead. Trial family: charges the M20 `sched_window_v1` calendar-anchored family (already registered 2026-07-22).

## DR-X8 — Academic (modality A) findings

### Verdict recommendation
**NOT-VIABLE-STRUCTURAL (confidence HIGH)** for any cost-floor *advantage* from maker-first intraday execution, and the academic evidence **CONFIRMS/EXHAUSTS-BY-FIELD** the M3 phantom-fill kill. On liquid names a passive fill earns ≈ the half-spread but simultaneously pays ≈ an equal-and-opposite adverse-selection drift, so the net "maker credit" is ≈ 0 (and turns negative once the $0.005/sh is added and non-fills are charged). Separately and decisively for M18: the "resting limit fills if the bar's low touches the price within 60 s" rule is the canonical touch=fill backtest fallacy — it credits fills that a back-of-queue retail order would not get (kiss-and-bounce) while the fills you *do* get are adversely selected, so a 90–98 % touch-fill rate is very likely a phantom-fill artifact. Our own structural exclusions (§1: no queue position, no maker rebates, 5–25 s manual latency) are exactly the capabilities the literature says the passive-capture value requires.

**What would flip it:** L3 queue-depth evidence (Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH / Databento XNAS-ITCH MBO) showing that for NVDA-class names a resting limit at the touch fills **>80 % within 60 s AND carries no adverse forward markout** — i.e., that the fills are not adversely selected. Every source fetched here contradicts that; observing it would overturn both the cost-floor and the M18 implication.

### Mechanism
There is **no price-insensitive payer** on the maker side of a continuous-market touch-fill — that is the finding. A resting limit is a free option written to the market: it is exercised (filled) precisely when the writer is wrong. A buy limit fills when a seller crosses to hit it, which happens disproportionately when price is continuing down (informed/momentum flow), and *fails* to fill when price ticks up and away (the outcome the trader wanted). The counterparty who fills you is therefore, on average, better-informed over the next few seconds; the "spread you earn" is compensation for exactly this adverse selection, and on liquid names the two roughly cancel. Queue-position value (the ability to be filled *before* the informed flow arrives and to jump ahead of the queue) is real but accrues only to the front of the queue — reachable via colocation, speed, and inverted-venue routing, none of which a manual $1k–$10k retail account controls. Persistence: structural (it is the equilibrium price of immediacy), so it does not decay. Capacity: irrelevant here — there is no edge to size.

### Claims
**C1 [CONFIRMED]** (T2, arXiv Jul-2024, sample 21 & 30 Nov 2023, 10-yr T-note futures, 1-s bars): a passive limit fill suffers a post-fill negative drift of ≈ −0.0065 price units ≈ **0.45 tick ≈ one half-spread** — i.e. the adverse-selection cost *nearly erases the half-spread the limit order is supposed to earn*; conditional fill is **≈100 % on adverse moves vs ≈1.8 % on favourable (kiss-and-bounce) moves**, blended ≈60 %. This IS the touch=fill trap quantified. Has a fill-probability cost model. FLAG: futures, not equities. — "The Negative Drift of a Limit Order Fill," arxiv.org/html/2407.16527v1
**C2 [CONFIRMED]** (T2, NASDAQ ITCH BAC, rev. Mar-2021): the correct fill rule is that a resting limit executes only if an incoming market order is **larger than the shares queued ahead of it** — a touch that does not trade *through* your size does not fill you; and "if a buy limit order doesn't get filled, the price has typically moved higher" (adverse selection stated explicitly for US equities). Directly refutes touch=fill. — "A Deep Learning Approach to Estimating Fill Probabilities in a LOB," business.columbia.edu deep-lob-2021.pdf; corroborated by backtest-realism consensus (T3) that touch=fill "assumes front-of-queue + ample volume … optimistic; with queue depth a limit fills only when prices move *beyond* its price."
**C3 [CONFIRMED]** (T2, Review of Finance 2026, sample May-2020, 300 US stocks incl. 100 large-cap, 27 M+ orders, 19 retail brokers, FINRA OATS): retail **non-marketable limit orders fill 65 %** overall (65.3 % large-cap) — **but over an average time-to-execution of 952 s (~16 min)** and average order duration 1,252 s; even *at-the-quote* orders average 106 s duration; the high fill rate is explicitly "a reward for patience." Only **7.4 % of retail limit orders execute-or-cancel-equivalent within 10 s and cumulative resolution is ~20 % by 1 min**. A 90–98 % fill *within 60 s* is inconsistent with the fill dynamics of even patient retail limit orders. — Anand-Samadi-Sokobin-Venkataraman "Retail Limit Orders," academic.oup.com/rof/article/30/2/459

### Economics sketch
**Maker-first cost floor on NVDA-class names.** NVDA effective spread ≈ 1–2 bp ⇒ half-spread earned on a passive fill ≈ **+0.5–1 bp**; adverse-selection drift on that fill ≈ **−0.5–1 bp** (C1 "≈ half-spread"; C8 adverse component dominates). Net spread capture ≈ **0 ± 0.5 bp before fees.** Subtract $0.005/sh = 0.38 bp/side on a $130 stock (0.77 bp round-trip) and charge the ~35 % non-fill opportunity cost, and the true maker-first floor is ≈ **−0.5 to −1 bp all-in — no better than, and plausibly worse than, the registered taker model ($0.005/sh + 0.5 bp taker slip).** The maker rebate that would offset this is not available to us (C9), and chasing it degrades fills anyway (C6). Net: **the cost model should NOT credit a maker fill with the half-spread; it should book ≈ 0 gross capture + $0.005/sh + an opportunity-cost/adverse-selection charge.**

**M18 ember.** Registered arithmetic: gross **+$1.79/trade** at 141 sh (~$18.3 k notional ≈ **+9.8 bps**); net **−$0.94** under registered fees; flips positive only if all-in cost < ~$1.27/side-equivalent. The +$1.79 gross is *contingent on the 90–98 % touch-fill rule.* Two corrections both cut it: (i) **fill-rate haircut** — the favourable (kiss-and-bounce) touches that a dip-buy signal *wants* fill at only single-digit-to-low-double-digit % in reality (C1 1.8 %; C3 sub-minute resolution ~20 %), so the profitable subset is systematically over-counted; (ii) **adverse-selection markout** — each realized fill carries ≈ 0.5–1 bp ≈ **$0.9–$1.8** of drift against the entry that the touch-at-limit-price sim omits (C1, C4). Together these plausibly revise the true gross down to ≈ **+$0 to +$1**, which *deepens* the −$0.94 rather than rescuing it. Champion comparison: **+2.5 bps/event dev, +12.5 bps holdout** — the champion survives precisely because it is a single-print **auction** fill (no queue, no touch ambiguity); M18's continuous-market touch-fill is the opposite structure.

**Mandated prior — touch-resting limits on NVDA-class names (60 s window, 1-s bars):**
- **Fill-rate prior:** the simulated 90–98 % is a phantom-fill artifact. Reasoned blended prior **≈ 20–50 % within 60 s**; on the profit-bearing (favourable-reversion) subset **≈ 2–15 %.** ⇒ overstatement ≈ **2–5× blended, ~10×+ on the subset that matters.** **EXPLICIT UNKNOWN:** no fetched source measures a $130 small-tick megacap at 1-s-bar/60-s touch granularity — this is an extrapolation from adjacent instruments (TY/BTC/ES/NQ futures, BAC, US-equity order-lifecycle data), not a direct measurement.
- **Adverse-selection prior:** ≈ **−0.5 to −1 bp per fill** (≈ half NVDA's spread), i.e. ~$0.9–$1.8 on M18's notional — comparable to the entire +$1.79 gross.
- **Implication:** treat the M18 ember as **most-likely-phantom until re-simulated under a queue-clearing fill rule with a forward markout charge.** This is the M3 phantom-fill scar recurring in a 60-second-touch guise.

Not OPEN-TESTABLE as an *edge* (verdict NOT-VIABLE-STRUCTURAL). But a cheap, **owned-data cost-model validation** is warranted and directly resolves the M18 implication:
- **Hypothesis:** M18's +$1.79 gross collapses toward ≤0 when touch=fill is replaced by a realistic fill rule.
- **Method / data (owned):** re-run M18's NVDA tape on Alpaca SIP **condition-coded** trades + NBBO (2024→2026 windows only, to avoid the legacy non-coded phantom-fill source). Compare fill rate and net P&L vs the touch-fill baseline.

## DR-X8 — Modality D (adversarial / prior-art) findings

### Verdict recommendation

**EXHAUSTED-BY-US (confidence HIGH for the rebate channel; MED-HIGH for the spread-saving channel).**
The "maker-first economics" premise splits into two channels and both are effectively closed for us. (a) **Maker-rebate channel — NOT-VIABLE-STRUCTURAL, HIGH:** at every commission/venue tier we can actually reach, a *successful* maker fill on our Nasdaq-listed names nets **zero (Alpaca, which keeps the rebate) or negative (IBKR, whose commission exceeds any accessible rebate)**; and on a ~$170 megacap the mandatory SEC+FINRA reg fees (~$0.0037/sh on the sell) already exceed the entire Nasdaq base add-rebate ($0.0013/sh Tape C). There is no accessible cell where we are "paid to make." (b) **Maker-entry-spread-saving channel — EXHAUSTED-BY-US, MED-HIGH:** this is *genuinely distinct in kind* from the dead M3 spread-capture family (M3 had zero signal; M18 overlays a maker entry on a directional trigger), but it was already tested end-to-end in M18 and landed **negative on every stream, every name** (−0.94 to −8.11 $/trade); its lone positive artifact (NVDA/MU mid-alpha +$2.19/+$2.15, gross +$1.79) is a fee-boundary ember, 1-of-4 names, post-hoc inside a 12-cell × ~160-trial family. Critically, M18's fill model is *optimistic* (90–98% touch fills, no adverse-selection markout), so correcting it can only make the economics **worse**, never rescue them. Neither the champion nor any survivor-profile logic supports a continuous-market maker structure — the champion survives precisely *because* it exits at the single-print cross with no maker/fill ambiguity.

**What would flip it:** A live M16-Phase-0 audit that measures, for touch-*resting* (not marketable) limits on our names at 5–25 s human latency, a realized fill rate ≥ ~80% **with near-zero adverse-selection markout** (fills not concentrated on adverse ticks; resting-limit E/Q ≤ ~0.6). That single result would mean the spread-saving channel is materially larger than the adverse-selection literature predicts, and a zero-commission (Alpaca) fee-boundary stream could plausibly clear net-positive. I expect the opposite, but that is the one cheap observation that changes the verdict.

### Mechanism

**Who pays a maker?** By construction, the counterparty who crosses the spread to hit your resting limit is the party who wants to trade *now* — disproportionately informed/urgent flow. So the maker's "payer" is adverse-selection flow, not a price-insensitive payer. This is the exact inverse of the champion's survivor logic (a named, price-insensitive indexed-MOC payer). A resting limit therefore has **no coherent price-insensitive payer** — the fills you get are selected for being the ones where the price is going through your level (you buy right before it keeps falling). That is a verdict input in itself: the structure fails survivor-profile point 3 outright.

**Why "maker-entry-on-signal" is distinct from dead M3 spread-capture — but non-rescuing.** M3 tried to earn the *spread itself* (no directional view); its entire edge was 100% adverse-selection-eaten (phantom fills; honest v1.4 fills ran −1.4 to −4.1 bps/plan). M18's structure is different: the alpha is the OU-reversion trigger's mid-drift, and the maker entry is a *cost-reduction overlay* (save the entry half-spread instead of paying it). Because the signal has (weak) mid-alpha, adversely-selected fills retain *some* of it — so they are not identical. But (i) the rebate sub-thesis is dead by fee arithmetic regardless of any signal, and (ii) the spread-saving sub-thesis, once actually run in M18, reduced losses but crossed zero on nothing; the realistic fill correction deepens the hole. So the premise is **distinct-in-kind yet empirically lands in the same negative-net place as M3**, and what remains is a **cost-model calibration question (M16 Phase 0), not a new alpha lane.**

**Capacity intuition:** irrelevant — the structure fails on cost/fill realism before capacity binds. At the $1k small-account lens it also fails on odd-lot mechanics (every megacap clip is a single/low-double-digit odd lot, non-protected under Reg NMS, historically non-displayed, behind all round-lot and hidden interest).

### Claims

**C1 [CONFIRMED]** (T1, Nasdaq price list accessed 2026-07-20; IBKR pricing accessed 2026-07-20): At tiers we can reach, **no net maker rebate exists** for us. Nasdaq base add-liquidity rebate for our Nasdaq-listed (Tape C) names is **$0.0013/sh** (Tape A/B $0.0018/sh); the top **$0.00305/sh** requires adding **>1.50% of total US consolidated volume** (billions of sh/mo — inaccessible). IBKR Tiered commission at our volume (<300k sh/mo) is **$0.0035/sh** — greater than *any* rebate we could obtain — so an IBKR maker fill nets **−$0.0022/sh** even after crediting the base rebate. — nasdaqtrader.com price list; interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php.

**C2 [CONFIRMED]** (T1, SEC FY2026 fee advisory 2026-04-04; FINRA TAF eff. 2026-01-01; arithmetic): **Mandatory reg fees exceed the whole base rebate on a high-priced megacap.** SEC Section 31 = **$20.60 per $1,000,000** of *sale* proceeds → on a $170 stock = **$0.0035/sh on the sell**; FINRA TAF = **$0.000195/sh on the sell** (cap $9.79). Round-trip reg ≈ **$0.0037/sh** > the $0.0013/sh Tape C base rebate (even ≈ $0.0026 if the rebate were earned on *both* legs, which our maker-entry/taker-exit structure never does). So even in the fantasy of zero commission + rebate-both-legs, reg fees wipe the rebate on our price points. — sec.gov/rules-regulations/fee-rate-advisories/2026-2; finra.org TAF.

**C3 [CONFIRMED]** (T2, arXiv 2502.18625 "The Market Maker's Dilemma," empirical, Binance BTC-perp Feb–Aug 2024, 232,897 maker orders — cross-asset *mechanism*, not an equity number): Passive fills are **adversely selected**. "Orders with negative subsequent five-second returns are highly likely to fill, whereas those with positive returns are much less likely to fill." Unconditional post-fill markout **≈ −0.8 bp (−0.3 bp net of rebate)**; back-of-queue fills **−0.775 to −1.157 bp**. This is the toxicity that makes "spread earned" partly illusory — and it is worst exactly at the touch/back-of-queue where a retail order sits.

**C4 [CONFIRMED]** (T2, Anand-Samadi-Sokobin-Venkataraman "Retail Limit Orders," Rev. of Finance 30(2):459, 27M retail orders, FINRA OATS, **May 2020 — a high-volatility month**): Retail limit orders placed **away from the NBBO fill ~50%**; near/not-aggressively-priced fill **~65%**. M18's registered **90.0/98.2/96.6/92.9%** touch-fill rates therefore overstate the *rate* by ≈**1.5–2×** before any adverse-selection adjustment; **~2/3 of retail limit executions occur riskless-principal** (wholesaler internalization), i.e., not by resting on an exchange and earning a rebate at all.

**C5 [CONFIRMED]** (T2, Battalio-Corwin-Jennings "Can Brokers Have It All?", J. Finance 71(5):2193, 2016; proprietary + TAQ data, ~2012 sample — DECAY-UNKNOWN post-2016): Chasing the maker rebate **worsens** execution — venues with the highest add-rebates show **lower fill rates and higher adverse-selection costs**; the inverted (low/negative-maker) venue had "lower adverse selection costs, faster fills, and higher fill rates." So the rebate is not free money; it is compensation for worse fills. Read via SSRN/journal summary (full tables not machine-fetchable — abstract-level confidence).

**C6 [CONFIRMED — the honest counter-anchor]** (T2, same Retail Limit Orders paper; and engineV5's +2.21 bps passive fact, condition-code ground-truthed internally): Passive/limit entry **does** have real value — retail limit orders cost **~10 bps less** than marketable orders on implementation shortfall (which already nets the opportunity cost of non-execution). **But** the advantage is **largest where spreads are wide, volatility high, and stocks small-cap — i.e., smallest on tight-spread liquid megacaps, our entire universe.** And the +2.21 bps / +10 bps figures are **per-FILLED-order** savings; they do **not** measure per-*intended*-signal economics (fill rate × adverse selection), which is what actually determines P&L and is unobservable in historical tick data.

**C7 [CONFIRMED]** (T1, SEC MDI/Reg-NMS odd-lot rules; effective ~2026-04-27): At the **$1k small-account lens every megacap clip is a pure odd lot** (NVDA ≈ 7 sh at $170; TSLA ≈ 3 sh; even the $10k research clip ≈ 58 sh is <100 = odd lot). Odd-lot quotes are **not protected under Reg NMS**, are **excluded from Rule 605 effective-spread**, and were historically **not displayed** (NBBO-improving odd lots only begin displaying under MDI rules April 2026). A resting odd-lot limit sits behind all round-lot and hidden interest → the maker-fill premise **collapses at deploy size**.

**C8 [CONFIRMED — PDT verdict]** (T1, SEC-approved FINRA rule eff. **2026-06-04**; cash-account rules): **PDT does not bind the small-account lens.** The $25k pattern-day-trader minimum and the PDT designation were **eliminated 2026-06-04**, and PDT never applied to cash accounts. The **real** small-account throttle is **T+1 settlement recycling**: at $1k (below the new **$2,000** intraday-margin threshold) the account behaves as cash-settled → ~**1 full-size round-trip/day** before unsettled-funds/good-faith-violation limits, capping the trade frequency any thin per-trade edge needs to compound. This vindicates the project's "no PDT constraint" note while relocating the true constraint to settlement + odd-lot mechanics.

**C9 [PLAUSIBLE]** (arithmetic on registered M18 numbers): At the $1k book the M18 gross ember (**+$1.79/trade at ~141 sh ≈ $24k notional**) scales to ≈ **+$0.09/trade at 7 sh**. That is below essentially every fixed cost: the IBKR **$0.35 order minimum** (×2 legs = $0.70) is ~8× the gross; even on zero-commission Alpaca, the per-trade human/bookkeeping/wash-sale-tracking burden (thousands of round-trips/yr on 5 repeated names → near-continuous wash-sale disallowance absent a §475(f) election) swamps $0.09 of gross. The ember is only *arithmetically* discussable at the $10k+ research clip; the small-account lens cannot realize it.

**C10 [UNVERIFIED-BY-DESIGN — the residual]** (synthesis of C3/C4/C6): The exact fraction of M18's touch-fill optimism that is real is **not resolvable from historical data** — you cannot observe whether *your* back-of-queue retail limit would have filled, nor the adverse selection on *your* fills. The gap between "per-filled saving" (+2.21 bps) and "per-intended economics" is the entire question, and it is a **measurement**, cheaply settled only by a live micro-order audit (M16 Phase 0). Combining C3+C4, the effective realizable maker benefit is plausibly **2–5× below** the M18 model (≈2× on fill rate × ≈1.5–2.5× on selection/markout) — consistent with the charge's hypothesis, but a literature-anchored prior, not a proof.

**Headline:** at every tier we can reach, a *successful* maker fill is net **zero (Alpaca) or negative (IBKR)**, and the regulatory floor alone (~$0.0037/sh) eats the whole capturable rebate on our price points. The "maker rebate" the M18 embers implicitly want is a mirage at our scale.

Effective maker benefit ≈ **2–5× below** M18's 90–98%/no-markout model. Practically: price the maker-entry benefit at **no more than ~40–50% of the modeled half-spread saving, minus ~0.5 bps adverse markout**, until measured.

**Comparison line:** champion (Nasdaq closing-auction basis) = **+2.5 bps/event dev, +12.5 bps/event holdout**, and it *never relies on a maker fill* — it exits at the single-print cross (survivor-profile 5/5). Maker-first intraday scores 0–1/5 and is strictly dominated on fill realism.

**PDT verdict (small-account lens):** PDT **does not bind** (designation + $25k minimum eliminated 2026-06-04; cash accounts always exempt). The binding small-account constraints are **T+1 settlement recycling** (sub-$2,000 balance → ~1 round-trip/day) and **odd-lot mechanics**, not PDT.

### Proposed next test

Not OPEN-TESTABLE as an alpha lane — verdict is EXHAUSTED-BY-US. The only warranted work is the **cost-model calibration already drafted as M16 Phase 0**, re-scoped to add one arm: alongside its marketable-clip E/Q buckets, log a **touch-*resting* (non-marketable) limit** arm — send a small resting limit at the bid/ask on NVDA/TSLA/AMD/MU, record (a) whether it fills within a fixed window, (b) the decision-time NBBO, (c) the 1–5 s post-fill mid markout. This is the **single cheapest measurement that settles the fill-realism question**: it directly observes our realized resting-fill rate and the adverse-selection markout on *our* orders at *our* latency — the two numbers that are unobservable in historical tick data and that separate the honest counter-anchor (+2.21 bps *per-filled*) from the per-*intended*-signal economics. Cost ≈ the same ~$10–20 in paid spreads already budgeted; charges no new alpha trial family (it is a cost-model measurement, not a strategy). **Kill/confirm rule:** resting-limit realized fill ≥ ~80% with markout ≥ −0.2 bps → spread-saving channel is real, reopen a fee-boundary registration on Alpaca; fill ≤ ~65% or markout ≤ −0.5 bps → freeze the adverse-selection prior above into the kernel and treat maker-first intraday as permanently closed.

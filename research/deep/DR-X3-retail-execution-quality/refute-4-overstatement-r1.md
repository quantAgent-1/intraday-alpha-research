# Refutation Pass — Claim Bundle X3-NEW-2 ("PI headlines overstate our reality")

## C-a: NBBO benchmark overstates PI by ~4x (Adams et al. / "Ernst ~400%")

**Refuted: FALSE (core claim stands), but with a material citation error and an overstated "4x" generalization.**

Both underlying papers exist and are genuine T2 sources:

1. **Adams, Samuel W., Connor Kasten, Eric K. Kelley (2024), "How Free is Free? Retail Trading Costs with Zero Commissions," Journal of Banking & Finance 165** (peer-reviewed, T2). SSRN 4403011 / ScienceDirect S0378426624001432. Diff-in-diff of effective spreads around the Oct-2019 zero-commission adoption wave; broad retail sample, not megacap-restricted. Finding, quoted via multiple independent secondary summaries (abstract-level access only — SSRN/ScienceDirect full text paywalled/Cloudflare-blocked): "NBBO-based price improvement measures consistently overstate economic savings, **in some subsamples by a factor of four or more**." This is a subsample maximum, not the paper's average/headline effect — I could not access the full text to confirm whether the driving subsample is wide-spread/low-priced/small-cap names (plausible given the mechanism — odd-lot/hidden liquidity is proportionally larger relative to wide quoted spreads — but NOT confirmed from primary text itself). Treat "up to ~4x" as accurate; treat "4x is the typical/average figure" as unsupported.

2. **NOT "Ernst."** The "~400%" figure traces to **Bradford (Lynch) Levy (2022), "Price Improvement and Payment for Order Flow: Evidence from a Randomized Controlled Trial,"** SSRN 4189658, publicized via Wharton WIFPR's own "Research Spotlight" (byline confirmed directly: "By Bradford (Lynch) Levy"). This is a working paper / RCT (T2, unpublished — cited by the SEC in its Reg NMS rulemaking, but not a peer-reviewed journal piece), not Thomas Ernst. Thomas Ernst is a real, distinct microstructure researcher (Ernst & Spatt on options PFOF/internalization, Ernst/Routledge/Spatt/Zetlin-Jones "Dark Crypto" 2026) — I could not locate any Ernst paper making the equities-NBBO-400% claim. **The claim bundle's "Ernst" attribution is a citation error; the correct author is Levy.**
   - Levy's RCT: trades "random stocks at random times" across a direct-access broker vs. TD Ameritrade/Robinhood. Direct orders beat NBBO by ~4bps on average. Levy's own stated conclusion: prior literature estimated PI at 5–9bps; correcting for the ~4bps NBBO bias implies true PI of ~1–5bps — i.e., an **average overstatement closer to ~2x**, with "as much as 400%" being the stated ceiling/extreme case, not the central estimate.

3. **Corroborating T2 source not in the original bundle**: Battalio & Jennings (2026), "Wholesaler Execution Quality," *Management Science* 72 (peer-reviewed), cited in Robert Battalio's May-2026 House Financial Services testimony (T1-adjacent, congressional record; primary text obtained and read in full via docs.house.gov PDF): for a May-2022 wholesaler sample, "the value of both price and size improvement provided by wholesalers to marketable orders... is **650% greater** than the value of price improvement documented in the mandatory Rule 605 execution quality reports." Different methodology, larger number, same direction — independently supports the general thesis that Rule-605/NBBO-based PI stats understate true economic value.

**Net: the general phenomenon (NBBO/605 benchmarks understate true PI) is real and corroborated by 3 independent T2/T1-adjacent sources. The specific "4x / 400%" figures are genuine but are subsample-maxima or extreme cases, not typical/average effects, and one of the two named authors ("Ernst") is simply wrong — the source is Levy.**

## C-b: honest E/Q ≈ 0.70–1.00 overall / 0.90–2.0 in 15:45–16:00 for liquid Nasdaq megacap odd-lots

**Refuted: TRUE.** No primary source measures E/Q for $400–$3,000 odd-lot marketable clips on NVDA/TSLA/AMD/MU/GOOGL-class names specifically in the 15:45–16:00 window, or even for that liquid-megacap bucket generally. The only located primary academic figures are from **Dyhrberg, Shkilko, Werner (2025), "The Retail Execution Quality Landscape," Journal of Financial Economics 168** (T2, peer-reviewed; Rule 605 sample ~2019–2022; abstract/summary-level access — SSRN/ScienceDirect both blocked full text, relied on a freely hosted conference-slide PDF search snippet plus multiple independent secondary citations for consistency): wholesaler-wide E/Q ≈ 0.76 (i.e., PI ≈ 24% of quoted spread on average across the pooled, small-cap-heavy sample), and an S&P-500-name PI figure of ~47% of spread, implying E/Q ≈ 0.53 for liquid names — both far below the claimed 0.70–1.00/0.90–2.0 range.

This project's own prior research memo (`modality-D.md` in this same folder) is explicit that the 0.70–2.0 range is **not** an empirical finding but the team's own **"Skeptic prior"** — an inference built by (a) taking one internal simulation data point (a single project close-window shortfall measurement implying E/Q≈2.0 at 15:55:10) and (b) stacking the Adams/Levy NBBO-haircut logic on top of the Dyhrberg S&P-name baseline, explicitly flagged there as "Confidence MED / partially UNKNOWN" with "no public megacap-odd-lot-at-close E/Q exists." That is inference-stacking of worst-case adjustments, self-labeled as such by the source document, not a primary-source-supported empirical range.

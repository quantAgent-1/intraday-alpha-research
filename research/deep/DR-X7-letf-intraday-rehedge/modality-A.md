## DR-X7 — Modality A (academic) findings

### Verdict recommendation
**EXHAUSTED-BY-FIELD (confidence MED-HIGH)** for the *intraday-reverting* single-stock-LETF-flow thesis that M19 would test. The best cost-serious academic evidence says the LETF re-hedge is a **close-window** event, not an intraday one ("the counterparties to leveraged ETFs unequivocally implement their portfolio rebalancing at the close … unrelated to intraday jumps … solely end-of-day"), and that the LETF price impact is **~2/3 permanent** — only ~a third reverses, and it reverses **overnight at the next open**, not intraday. The channel that *does* revert cleanly (84%) is the *options-gamma* channel, which is a different payer and already GEX-blocked for us. The one LETF expression the literature supports is **close-window continuation** (trade *with* the flow into the cross) — i.e. exactly the champion / M12 Cell A we already own. This reinforces (does not merely echo) our own **EXHAUSTED-BY-US** receipt: M18 already killed unconditional and conditioned intraday reversion on NVDA/TSLA/AMD/MU (obituary 2026-07-20; embers fee-killed). Structurally the intraday expression is also a continuous-market single-name taker trade — the dead-list profile.

**What would flip it:** direct, PIT-clean evidence — from a post-2022 single-stock complex specifically — that the swap desk hedges a *measurable* fraction of the delta **intraday** (not at the close) AND that this specific flow **reverts within the session** net of taker cost on a mega-cap. No published paper shows this; index-LETF evidence and our own M18 both point the other way.

### Mechanism
Who pays: LETF investors (mostly retail) bear a mandatory daily re-leveraging cost; the **price-insensitive executor** is the swap counterparty (per the GraniteShares prospectus, Cowen Financial Products LLC / TD) that must restore notional delta to the leverage target measured **close-to-close** ("from the close of regular trading on one trading day to the close on the next"). Because the fund contract mandates the reset and tracking-error tolerance is tight, the counterparty trades *with* the day's move regardless of price. Why it persists: the reset lands in the **closing auction / last minutes** — a scheduled single-print with no continuous-market fill ambiguity; anticipators can front-run the *direction* but the flow still must clear at the close. Capacity is bounded by leverage-adjusted AUM × the day's return.

Crucially for THIS charge, that mechanism produces **close-window pressure that is directional/momentum-like and mostly permanent** — it does **not** describe an intraday-reverting payer. For the intraday-reversion hypothesis specifically, **no coherent intraday-reverting LETF payer is found**: the mechanical flow is not intraday, and what intraday delta-management the swap desk does is, by theory and by the prospectus's silence, spread/hidden to *minimize* impact. The reverting force in the literature is options market-makers' gamma hedging (a separate, GEX-degraded channel), and even that reverts overnight, not intraday.

### Claims
C1 [CONFIRMED] (T2, Barbon-Beckmeyer-Buraschi-Moerke 2021, LETF sample **Jan 2012–Dec 2019**): LETF hedge flow is executed **at the close**, "unequivocally," and is "unrelated to intraday jumps … takes place solely end-of-day"; they establish this by sliding the hedging window across 10:00–16:00. Options-MM hedging, by contrast, is partly intraday (near-immediate after early jumps). — *fmai.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/Derivatives2021/beckmeyer_etf_options.pdf*

C2 [CONFIRMED] (T2, same paper): The end-of-day LETF price impact is **~2/3 permanent** — "on average 84% of the impact of delta-hedging and **a third of the impact of leveraged ETF rebalancing is reversed at the next open**" (open = close→10:00 next day). LETF momentum coefficient at the open −45.96 (t −2.11). So the LETF channel is momentum/continuation; the strongly-reverting channel is options, overnight. Gross figures, NO cost applied to the reversal test.

C3 [CONFIRMED] (T2, same paper): Their tradeable LETF expression is **close-window continuation, not reversion** — rank stocks by rebalancing demand at 15:30, long top / short bottom decile, hold **15:30→16:00**. HmL (LETF signal): equal-weight **11.82%/yr, Sharpe 4.65, 60.8% hit**; **value-weight 5.78%/yr, Sharpe 2.51, 56.3% hit**; combined HP+LETF daily = **+4.36 bps/day GROSS** (NO-COST-MODEL). Performance is **halved value-weighted** — "the effect of leveraged ETF rebalancing is outsized for **smaller** stocks," a headwind for a mega-cap single-stock application (NVDA/TSLA).

C4 [CONFIRMED] (T2, same paper): The flow proxy is **exactly the M12/F formula and is PIT** — leverage-adjusted AUM ≡ AUM·L·(L−1), interacted with the **benchmark return since the prior close to 15:30** (their `rpre`). This pins the r-window the desk targets (**since-prior-close, close-to-close**) and confirms F_t is computable live from T-1 AUM anchors × live return.

C5 [CONFIRMED] (T1, GraniteShares Funds Prospectus, **Mar 17 2025**; 1.25x TSLA / 2x NVDA / COIN / META / GOOGL etc.): Exposure is reset "**at the end of each trading day**" to the leverage multiple of NAV, single day defined "**from the close … to the close on the next trading day**"; obtained **primarily via swaps** (counterparty Cowen Financial Products LLC, a TD subsidiary), 30–45% collateral posted; indirect swap cost ≈ **1.00%/yr**. The reset target is close-to-close; the swap desk's **intraday** hedge timing is its own discretion and is **not disclosed**.

C6 [CONFIRMED] (T2, Tuzun, Fed FEDS 2013-48 / SSRN 2190708, sample **2008–09 & 2H-2011**): a **1% broad-index move induces ≈ $1.04 bn** of LETF rebalancing flow; "price-insensitive and concentrated trading … results in price reaction and extra volatility." Frames the payer as price-insensitive and concentrated (i.e., at the close) — but is a volatility/financial-stability result, not an intraday-reversion tradeable.

C7 [CONFIRMED] (T2, Cheng & Madhavan 2009; and Jain-Mishra-Pagano-Rodriguez "Seesaws and Swings," EFMA 2024): LETF rebalancing is **same-direction as the day's return** → end-of-day **positive feedback / momentum** amplification (Sorkin anecdote: LETF demand drove a 1% S&P run-up in the **last 18 minutes** on 2011-10-10). Reinforces momentum-not-reversion for the LETF channel.

C8 [PLAUSIBLE] (T2, QFE 2024 survey "The market impact of leveraged ETFs: A Survey of the literature"): statistical associations between LETF rebalancing demand and late-day returns/volatility are robust, but "**most … papers suffer from potentially serious methodological errors, and the economic associations appear to be insignificant**"; the market absorbs the demand with "insignificant degradation of market quality." Adversarial anchor: LETF flow's *standalone* economic impact is contested (this is the already-DEAD `letf_window` family, not the champion's cross-sectional selector).

C9 [PLAUSIBLE, abstract-only] (T2, Bessembinder 2025, SSRN 5369417, single-stock LETFs since **2022**): long levered single-stock ETFs underperform ≈ **0.79%/month** (≈0.26% daily-rebalancing + ≈0.53% frictions); worse in high-vol regimes partly because those regimes carry **return reversals** in the underlying. NOTE: measures the *ETF investor's own decay*, **not** the fund's hedging **impact on the underlying's intraday price** — the single-stock intraday-impact-on-underlying question is **unpublished (UNKNOWN in the field)**.

C10 [UNVERIFIED for daily LETF hedge] (T2, Wang-Yao-Yelekenova SSRN 4324054, sample **2005–2020**, via Duke FinReg blog; + VIX-products paper JBF S0378426621002363, abstract-only): predictable-flow **anticipation is already an established trade** — hedge funds front-run **scheduled index reconstitution** (+0.86%/mo pre-event); and leveraged-VIX rebalancing's late-day impact is "**fully reversed overnight**." But these concern *monthly reconstitution* and *VIX futures*, not *daily single-stock equity LETF* hedging; the general literature theme is that such anticipation is competed toward zero **net of cost** as arbitrageurs learn. Why the *close-window* expression survives while the *intraday/anticipatory* one gets arbitraged: the close leg executes in a **single-print auction** against a **mandated** payer (no fill ambiguity), whereas the intraday leg is a continuous-market race.

### Economics sketch
Cited gross for the *close-window continuation* leg (the only LETF expression with academic support): Barbon et al. HmL LETF strategy = **+4.36 bps/day GROSS**, but that is a *fully cross-sectional dollar-neutral decile book* (dozens of optionable∩LETF names traded 15:30→16:00 in the continuous market, **no cost model**, effect **halved on large caps**) — untradeable by a retail hand-keyer and not a single-name event. Our cost burden for an *intraday-reversion* single-name taker on NVDA/TSLA at $10k notional: full spread + impact each way on ~20 bps/event noise; M18 already measured the NVDA/MU embers (mid-alpha +2.19 / +2.15 $/trade) as **fee-killed** — net prior ≈ **≤ 0**. Comparison line: **champion = +2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 holdout** (per-event, single-print auction) — the LETF mechanism's *only* profitable footprint is in that same close/auction slot, already harvested.

**(a) Timing-split verdict:** For the mechanical daily-reset flow, **≈ 0% intraday / ≈ 100% close-window** on the best (index-LETF) evidence — "solely end-of-day, unrelated to intraday jumps" (Barbon et al.), consistent with the prospectus's close-to-close reset. Any continuous intraday delta-management by the single-stock swap desk is **UNDISCLOSED and UNMEASURED in the literature**; the single-stock-specific split is formally **UNKNOWN**, but the prior is strong close-concentration. It is **not** threshold-triggered for the LETF channel (jumps don't move LETF hedging intraday; that's the options channel).

**(b) PIT intraday F-state — YES (computable) / but immaterial.** F_t = L(L−1)·AUM(T-1 anchor)·r_t is exactly Barbon et al.'s leverage-adjusted-AUM × `rpre`, and the r-window the reset targets is **since-prior-close** — so the *state* is PIT-computable live. BUT computability of the state ≠ existence of intraday *pressure* from it: the documented pressure lands at the close, so a PIT intraday F-state is a **model assumption about un-disclosed desk timing**, not a disclosed intraday schedule.

**(c) Does M19 deserve registration? — Honestly, no, not as an intraday-reversion family.** The academic case against is threefold and independent: the flow is close-concentrated (C1), its impact is ~2/3 permanent with the reverting third landing overnight in the options channel (C2, C7), and the survey literature already doubts LETF flow's standalone economic significance (C8) — plus our own M18 killed intraday reversion on these exact four names and the embers were fee-killed. Registering M19 as intraday reversion would re-fight dead M18 and the dead intraday payer-detector family without a new mechanism or data class. The one genuinely *unpublished* gap — whether a *single-stock* swap desk leaves a measurable *intraday* footprint that reverts intraday — is testable on owned data, but its prior is poor and, even if a footprint exists, capturing it is a continuous-market taker trade that fails gates 1/5/7 at our size. The defensible use of this mechanism is what M12 already does: a **close-window, name-selectivity** signal feeding the auction champion — not a new intraday-reversion stream. Recommend **do not register M19-as-reversion**; if anything is registered, it should be a *close-window* single-stock flow-state refinement charged to the champion family, not a new intraday reversion family.

### Proposed next test
Not OPEN-TESTABLE as an intraday-reversion family (verdict EXHAUSTED-BY-FIELD, reinforced EXHAUSTED-BY-US via M18 and NOT-VIABLE-STRUCTURAL on gates 1/5). **Coverage note / the one low-prior diagnostic that would settle it before any registration:** using owned 1-s OFI/microprice bars for NVDA/TSLA/AMD/MU plus 1-min LETF bars and the F_t = L(L−1)·AUM·r state, run a *descriptive, non-trading* attribution — regress intraday signed OFI in the underlying on the live F_t state within 30-min buckets across the session, to measure what fraction of the day's hedge footprint (if any) is detectable **before** 15:45 vs concentrated in the last 15 min, and whether high-|F_t| intraday moves revert by the close net of the half-spread. If (and only if) that diagnostic shows a *material* pre-15:45 footprint that reverts intraday net of taker cost — contradicting Barbon et al. — would an intraday family warrant registration; the a-priori expectation from all evidence above is a null (footprint concentrated in the final minutes, impact continuing into the cross, not reverting). This is a mechanism-prior probe, not a strategy — consistent with the house doctrine that conditioning hypotheses require demonstrated conditional mid-alpha first.

## DR-X11 — Opening-cross imbalance residue (quantity axis), modality A (academic) findings

### Verdict recommendation
**OPEN-TESTABLE (confidence LOW)** — The exact F3 cell (unpaired-QUANTITY residue at ~09:28:30 → same-direction *continuation* to ≤11:00 in liquid US names) is $0-testable on owned data and has not been directly tested-and-killed in the published record, so I cannot honestly stamp "field-exhausted" on the precise construction. BUT modality A found **zero prior art supporting the continuation direction** and a robust, multi-source **reversal / mean-reversion prior**: the one direct study of US opening-auction imbalance (Challet–Gourianov) finds the imbalance itself is mechanically mean-reverting with no established post-open return predictability, and three independent canonical results (Amihud–Mendelson, Berkman et al., Cushing–Madhavan) find opening/auction *price pressure reverses*, not continues. No coherent price-insensitive payer exists for a *continuation* signal in liquid megacaps. Net: register (if at all) as a **low-prior "close-the-quantity-axis" receipt** parallel to how M24 closed the price axis — not as a promising edge — and pre-commit the *reversal/fade* direction as the higher-prior alternative. Survivor score ≈ 2/5.

**What would flip it:** a modern (2016+) peer-reviewed or SSRN study directly showing that opening-auction *imbalance quantity* (unpaired shares), net of the indicative-price dislocation, predicts **same-sign** post-open drift over 30–120 min in liquid US equities with a cost model. I searched for this specifically and did not find it; its absence is the load-bearing finding.

### Mechanism
**No coherent payer found for CONTINUATION.** For unpaired residue to predict same-direction drift, the residue must proxy *persistent, price-insensitive* demand that keeps pushing price *after* the auction has already cleared it at a market-clearing price. The mechanics cut the other way:
- **The auction clears the imbalance at a price.** A genuinely informed imbalance is impounded into the *cross print* (that is the auction's function); systematic post-cross drift then requires the auction to *under-react*, i.e. slow information diffusion at the open. Slow diffusion is implausible in NVDA/TSLA/AMD-class names and is the intraday-PEAD-on-megacaps mechanism already on the field dead-list (§5).
- **Opening imbalance is mechanically mean-reverting** (Challet–Gourianov): incoming pre-cross orders *oppose* the standing imbalance 50–65% of the time, so the disseminated 09:28:30 residue is already being arbitraged toward zero before the bell. What survives to the cross is the *un-absorbed* remainder — more likely noise/liquidity than persistent information.
- **The identifiable at-open flows reverse.** Retail attention-driven buying (Berkman et al.) inflates the open then reverses over the session; opening prices carry a transitory/noise component (Amihud–Mendelson) that reverses over the first hour; MOC/index rebalance flow — the champion's price-insensitive payer — is concentrated at the *close*, not the open, and open MOO interest is largely *paired* at the cross.
- The only surviving directional story is thus **fade/reversal**, which (a) on the price axis is internal M24 (closed, between-the-bars), (b) on the retail-attention axis is Berkman (published/known and largely arbitraged in megacaps), and (c) at the close is BANNED in this program.

### Claims
C1 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2018, sample 2010-01→2016-11 +suppl. to 2018): In US opening auctions (NYSE/NASDAQ/Arca) the indicative match price is "strongly mean-reverting because the imbalance is"; new pre-cross orders oppose the standing imbalance ~50–65% of the time; the paper establishes **no** predictability of post-open returns from opening-imbalance magnitude (focus is pre-auction dynamics). Opening auctions ≈ 2–3% of daily volume. NO-COST-MODEL. — Challet & Gourianov, *Dynamical regularities of US equities opening and closing auctions*, arXiv:1802.01921. https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.01921
C2 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2012, ~13 yrs US intraday, ≈1996–2008): Positive overnight returns are followed by **intraday reversals** driven by an opening price high relative to intraday prices, concentrated in retail-attention stocks with net retail buying at the open; the implicit cost of buying at the open "frequently exceeds the effective half spread." Direction of open buying pressure = **REVERSES**, in the F3 holding window. — Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang, *Paying Attention: Overnight Returns and the Hidden Cost of Buying at the Open*, JFQA 47(4). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1625495
C3 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 1987, NYSE early-1980s): Open-to-open returns show **higher variance and more-negative serial autocorrelation** than close-to-close; opening prices contain a transitory (noise) component that **reverses** over the first hour of trading. Foundational "noisy-open → reversal" result. DECAY-UNKNOWN post-2016. — Amihud & Mendelson, *Trading Mechanisms and Stock Returns*, J. Finance 42(3). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1987.tb04567.x
C4 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2000, Russell 1000, Jun-1997→Jul-1998): The named "Cushing–Madhavan class" is a **CLOSE** study; MOC order-imbalance indications are followed by **systematic return REVERSALS** consistent with temporary price pressure from liquidity trading — i.e., the seminal auction-imbalance result is reversal, not continuation, and is at the (banned) close. — Cushing & Madhavan, *Stock returns and trading at the close*, J. Financial Markets 3(1) 45-67. https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v3y2000i1p45-67.html
C5 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2010, US ~2001–2005): Intraday **return continuation** exists (half-hour intervals at exact multiples of a trading day, ≥40 days) BUT "volume, **order imbalance**, volatility and bid-ask spreads … do **not** explain the return patterns." So the one robust intraday-continuation result is periodicity/seasonality, explicitly NOT driven by order imbalance. — Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka, *Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns*, J. Finance 65(4) 1369-1407. https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3535
C6 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2026 JFQA / SSRN 2022): Price impact is **smallest in the closing auction** (~2–15 bps annualized cost for low-turnover anomaly strategies; ~9–21 bps ex-microcap); **"opening auctions are illiquid"** and the continuous market around the open is the costlier venue. Cost realism for anything transacting near the open. — Goyal & Jegadeesh, *Price Impact in Closing Auctions, Opening Auctions, and Continuous Markets*, JFQA. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4300417
C7 [PLAUSIBLE] (T2 working paper, 2026, US+cross-asset 1993–2026): At short lags, equity return predictability is in **MAGNITUDE, not SIGN** — "a large return … predicts a smaller return today regardless of sign — the fingerprint of bid-ask bounce and non-synchronous trading, not directional reversal." Modern caution: opening-imbalance *size* most likely predicts subsequent *volatility*, not *direction*. Working-paper status; flagged. — Portnaya, *The Bounce Has No Direction*, arXiv:2606.29591. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29591
C8 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2004, NYSE 1988–1998): Lagged **continuous-market** daily order imbalance predicts returns with short-run continuation that **subsequently reverses** (inventory/price-pressure); this is a daily continuous-market result, NOT auction residue, and includes no auction/open test. Background only. — Chordia & Subrahmanyam, *Order imbalance and individual stock returns: Theory and evidence*, J. Financial Economics 72(3) 485-518. https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v72y2004i3p485-518.html
C9 [CONFIRMED] (T2, 2008/2000): Consolidating order flow in the opening auction **improves price discovery** (Barclay–Hendershott–Jones 2008); pre-cross Nasdaq price discovery via quote signalling, opening prices "noisy and inefficient before 1997" (Cao–Ghysels–Hatheway). Establishes the open as an *efficiency-improving* venue — arguing the cross impounds information rather than leaving exploitable residue. Dated (pre-modern-cross). — BHJ, J. Financial Markets 2008; Cao, Ghysels & Hatheway, *Price Discovery Without Trading*, SSRN 183409.
C10 [CONFIRMED — coverage/gap finding] (T1/T2, searches 2016→2026): I found **no** published US study (peer-reviewed, SSRN, or arXiv q-fin) that isolates opening-auction *imbalance QUANTITY* (net of indicative-price dislocation) as a predictor of **same-sign** post-open drift at 5–120 min in the modern (2016+) market. The literature is either (a) reversal-signed, (b) closing-auction, (c) continuous-market, or (d) imbalance-does-not-explain-continuation. The absence of a positive continuation result across a broad search is itself the verdict input.

### Economics sketch
- **Expected gross (continuation):** no cited positive; academic prior ≈ 0 or negative. The only sizeable *at-open* effect in the literature is a **reversal** (Berkman: open-buying reverses intraday), concentrated in low-liquidity attention names and largely arbitraged out of megacaps — and it is a *fade*, the opposite of F3's stated direction.
- **Our cost burden:** taker RT ≈ 2–5 bps megacaps, up to ~16 bps mid-liquidity semis, transacted in the first 30–90 min where continuous-market spreads/impact are highest (C6: "opening auctions are illiquid," continuous-open is the costly venue).
- **Net prior:** ≤ 0 for the continuation leg. Even a real *reversal* leg would fight elevated first-hour costs and overlaps closed/known work (M24 price axis; Berkman attention).
- **Comparison line:** champion = **+2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 holdout**. F3-continuation has no cited gross to place against this; the honest prior sits at or below zero.

### Proposed next test (OPEN-TESTABLE — framed as a cheap close-the-axis receipt, NOT a promising edge)
- **Hypothesis (pre-registered, two-sided):** H1c (continuation, low-prior): sign(unpaired imbalance_shares at last pre-cross 09:28:30–09:29:59 snapshot) predicts **same-sign** open(09:35)→11:00 return; H1r (reversal, higher-prior per C2/C3/C4): predicts **opposite-sign** return. Residualize the quantity signal against the near-vs-ref **price** dislocation (the M24 axis, already closed) so the test isolates *quantity* incremental content.
- **Named payer:** none identified for continuation (this is why the prior is low); reversal payer = transitory opening noise / retail-attention over-pricing (Amihud–Mendelson, Berkman).
- **Data:** owned — XNAS.ITCH NOII open-window messages (imbalance_shares/paired_shares/near/far/ref) + 1-s XNAS top-of-book (10 names) for entry/exit marks. $0. No purchase.
- **Universe:** the 33 owned Nasdaq names; core-5 (NVDA/TSLA/AMD/MU/GOOGL) 2020→2026 as the powered subset, broad 28 as frozen OOS.
- **Expected n & power:** ≥250 events across ≥150 sessions easily reached; per-event sd ≈ 20 bps → SE of the mean over n=250 ≈ 1.3 bps, adequate to reject a ≥4 bps effect (the ≥2× megacap-cost bar) at conventional power.
- **A-priori thresholds:** promote only if the *quantity-residual* leg (post price-dislocation control) shows |mean| ≥ 4 bps/event net of a 3 bps taker stress, one-sided p<0.01, AND survives ex-top-5-session drop (concentration trap, §7) AND holds sign on the frozen 28-name OOS.
- **Kill criteria:** continuation mean ≤ 0 or not distinguishable from the price-axis (M24) residual → **kill the continuation leg**; if only the *reversal* leg is significant, route to synthesis as "quantity axis = reversal, redundant with M24/Berkman" rather than a new registration.
- **Trial family charged:** the F-family (opening-cross reframing of open question #10) — the SAME multiple-testing family as M6b/M24; this is one more sub-test, not a new family.

## DR-X11 — Nasdaq opening-cross imbalance residue (quantity axis): adversarial / prior-art findings

### Verdict recommendation
**EXHAUSTED-BY-FIELD (confidence MED)** — and, on the specific payer mechanism, additionally **NOT-VIABLE-STRUCTURAL**.
The megacap-continuation core of F3 is killed by convergent T1/T2 evidence: (a) the *mechanism* — unpaired on-open interest CANCELS at the 09:30 cross (T1 Rule 4752), so there is no hidden residue that "completes after the open" to pay continuation; (b) the *direction* — every documented opening/auction inefficiency at the megacap scale is a REVERSAL (auction deviations revert ~110% in large caps; the surviving open-auction edge is a retail→wholesaler reversal captured by internalizers), not the continuation F3 posits; (c) the *decay/arb-speed* — order-imbalance return predictability declined secularly to near-random-walk with tighter ticks and more liquidity, and the NOII signal is public, ~$15–199/mo, and consumed within seconds by colocated HFT — the textbook competed-away setup. The one honest seam: **no published source tests F3's *exact* construction** (unpaired *quantity* at 09:28:30 → continuation, open side, quote legs), so this is a field-consensus kill of the mechanism and the megacap direction, not a direct obituary of F3-as-specified. F3's own author prior (P(pass) 0.05–0.08) and our M24 result (megacap fade −19.6 bps = efficient; broad +15.4 but a *price*-dislocation fade, family closed BETWEEN THE BARS) point the same way. Survivor-profile score **1/5**.
**What would flip it:** a documented, cost-realistic, post-2016 test showing unpaired opening-cross *quantity* (not price dislocation) predicts *continuation* in *liquid* names, net of ≥5 bps taker cost, entering at manual (5–25 s) latency ≥ 09:35 — none found in ≥21 searches.

### Mechanism
The F3 payer story is: unpaired shares at the cross must "still get done" and pay for it in continuous trading after 09:35, so the residual-quantity sign predicts continuation. **This story is structurally wrong.** Under Nasdaq Rule 4752, the unpaired portion of the opening imbalance is composed of on-open order types (MOO/LOO/OIO/Early-Market-Hours) plus eligible continuous-book interest. At the cross: the *on-open* orders that do not execute are **cancelled and returned to the entering firm** — they evaporate, they do not become post-open market pressure; the *continuous-book* orders that did not execute simply **remain in the visible displayed book** everyone already sees — no hidden residue. So the reported "imbalance_shares" quantity does not survive the cross as latent directional demand. What information the pre-cross imbalance *did* carry is priced into the 09:30 print itself (the indicative price mean-reverts toward the LOB mid as the imbalance decays into the cross), and any remaining inefficiency documented in the literature is a **reversal**, with the profit captured by wholesalers/HFT who see the same feed faster. No coherent *price-insensitive continuation payer* survives contact with the venue mechanics.

### Economics sketch
- **Expected gross:** For megacaps, the field says the open-imbalance signal is efficiently priced (C4,C6,C7) — residual continuation ≈ 0; the only documented open inefficiency is a *reversal* already captured by wholesalers (C5). Our own M24 diagnostic agrees: champion-5 fade −19.6 bps (efficient). Broad names carried +15.4 bps in M24, but that was a *price-dislocation fade* (near-vs-ref), not quantity-continuation, and the family closed BETWEEN THE BARS.
- **Cost burden:** continuous-market taker round-trip 2–5 bps (megacap) to ~16 bps (semis), plus 5–25 s slippage into a fast 09:35 tape and post-promo fees as a stress annotation.
- **Net prior:** ≤ 0 for megacaps; for broad names any plausible gross is consumed by the ~16 bps RT floor. F3 is the *inverse* structure of the champion — two continuous-market spread crossings chasing a signal the field says is gone by the cross.
- **Comparison line:** champion = **+2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 holdout** (single-print auction exit, no exit spread, scheduled instant). F3 offers a worse execution structure against a weaker, decayed, wrong-signed signal.

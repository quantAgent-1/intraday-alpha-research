### Mechanism
No coherent price-insensitive payer was found specific to the **opening** side. This is the load-bearing negative finding of this lane. Contrast with the close: multiple independent sources (institutional market-structure commentary encountered incidentally across several practitioner pages) converge on the fact that index funds, ETFs, and systematic institutional rebalancing overwhelmingly target the **closing** print for NAV/benchmark-tracking reasons — that is the champion's mechanism, and it is also why closing-imbalance strategies are the well-trodden practitioner genre (see C5). No equivalent "must complete today, price-insensitive, opening-specific" institutional flow surfaced in any source reviewed.

The closest thing to a named mechanism on the **open** side is retail MOO/LOO order flow (C1, Brown 2025): uninformed retail demand concentrates at the open (via zero-commission apps routing market orders as MOO/LOO), wholesalers/auction participants under-anticipate it, and price temporarily overshoots — but the documented resolution of that overshoot is **reversal**, not continuation. That is a real, coherent, price-insensitive-ish payer (uninformed retail, not mechanical index flow) — but it argues for fading the imbalance, not riding it, which is the opposite of F3's stated hypothesis. Separately, Challet & Gourianov (C2) find that in the pre-print window itself, sophisticated participants place orders opposing the *current* imbalance ~95% of the time — i.e., much of any "residue" is already actively arbitraged before the cross ever prints, which cuts against the idea that a clean, uninformed, unexecuted quantity signal survives into continuous trading at 09:35.

Capacity intuition: nobody sized this. No practitioner source gave a magnitude, so there is no basis to estimate capacity; the champion's own MU-concentration caveat (§3 of brief) is a cautionary analog for how thin these effects can get once you leave the single largest driver name.

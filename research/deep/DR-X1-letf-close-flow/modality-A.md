## DR-X1 — A academic findings
### Verdict recommendation
**OPEN-TESTABLE** (confidence **MED**) — Academic LETF literature (2009→2024) confirms a coherent price-insensitive rebal payer that concentrates **late-day** (last ~30–60 min; often framed vs MOC volume) via the Cheng–Madhavan identity \(\Delta I \approx m(m-1)\,r\,A \equiv (L^2-L)\cdot\mathrm{AUM}\cdot r_\mathrm{day}\), with swap-counterparty hedges transmitting into underlyings/futures. Early samples report large late-day return/vol co-movement; better-identified and more recent work finds **economically small** average impact on liquid large-caps after capital flows, short-lived reversal, and **no net-of-cost profitable front-run** of rebal. **Single-stock LETF rebal impact 2022–2026 is essentially absent from peer-reviewed evidence** — a gap, not a kill. Continuous intraday LETF-window detectors are **EXHAUSTED-BY-US (M3/M5)** and match the literature’s “near close only” timing; the open cell is **close-auction / late-day expression + name ranking by predicted flow intensity** (NVDA complex vs TSLA ≫ AVGO/NFLX/LRCX), testable on owned bars + cheap AUM history.
What would flip it: A T2 (or our Stage-A) result that single-stock + index LETF predicted flow ranks **do not** match late-day/NOII pressure on NVDA-class names (mismatch kill), or that close-conditioned economics are ≤0 net after entry burden at our size on n≥250 high-flow events.
### Mechanism
**Who pays:** LETF sponsors (and swap dealers hedging total-return swaps) must restore target daily leverage \(m\) at end of day. Both leveraged and inverse funds rebalance **in the same direction as the day’s return** on the referenced asset (longs buy after up days / sell after down; inverse reduce shorts after up / add after down). Flow is formulaic and non-discretionary once \(r\) and AUM are known → classic price-insensitive payer. Capital creations/redemptions (typically known by ~15:30) can **offset** a large fraction of gross rebal demand (Ivanov–Lenkey).
**Why near the close:** Rebal must pin to the daily close/NAV reference; early rebal risks reverse path risk. Empirics: measurable association with **last-hour / last-30-min** returns and volatility; Barbon et al. find LETF-related pressure only in the **final** rebal window (unlike option γ-hedgers, who time earlier). Sponsors may submit **MOC** to brokers through the day; brokers can hedge earlier → mix of continuous late RTH + auction, not pure OTC silence. Futures (index LETFs) and cash underlyings both absorb hedges.
**Persistence / capacity:** Predictable mechanical flow + growing LETF AUM (survey: ~$3B 2006 → >$80B global ~2023) argue for persistence; rising close-auction liquidity (Bogousslavsky–Muravyev: auction ~3.1%→7.5% of daily $ volume 2010–2018) and learning by LPs **compress** impact over time. Single-stock LETFs concentrate 100% of rebal on one name (vs index weight \(w_i\)), so **name-level intensity can exceed classic index-LETF studies** even if market-wide effects stay small — that is the 2022–2026 open mechanism, not re-derivation of 2009–2014 cascade debate.
### Claims
C1 [CONFIRMED] (T2, JOIM 2009 / SSRN 2010, snapshot Feb 2009 AUM of 84 US equity LETFs): Rebal demand \(\Delta I = m(m-1)rA\); at 1% (5%) common index move, aggregate rebal ≈ **$787M ($3.93B)** ≈ **16.8% (50.2%) of median MOC volume** — **NO-COST-MODEL**, counterfactual (no capital flows; perfect cross-index correlation) → overstates volume. Frames rebal as **MOC-relevant**, not continuous open. — Cheng & Madhavan, *The Dynamics of Leveraged and Inverse ETFs*; https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1539120
C2 [CONFIRMED] (T2, Fed FEDS 2013-48 / Tuzun 2014, sample Jun 2006–Dec 2011, 58 US equity LETFs): 1% broad-index move → ~**$1.04B** rebal (~0.84% of US stock volume); late-day (15:00–16:00) return bias **~5.5–12.9 bps** (mid-cap→financial) and late-day vol add **~22.7–50.5 bps** under 1% common move — **NO-COST-MODEL**; no capital-flow adjust; misassigned index maps for many funds (Lenkey critique). Rebal described as **last-hour**. Overnight/next-day partial reversal. — Tuzun, *Are Leveraged and Inverse ETFs the New Portfolio Insurers?*; https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201348/201348pap.pdf
C3 [CONFIRMED] (T2, J. Financial Markets 2018, sample Jun 2006–May 2014, 72 US equity LETFs / 26 indexes): Capital flows **cut** rebal demand sharply (e.g. on largest-|r| quintile: −85% for +3x, −66% for +2x); after flow-adjust + risk factors, share of late-day returns (vol) attributable to rebal ≈ **0.5–1.8% (1.2–6.6%)** → average late-day return impact **≪ 1 bps** (late-day |r| typically <50 bps) and vol impact **≲ 6 bps** — **economically insignificant** though statistically significant on millions of obs. Late-day cutoffs 15:00–15:50 similar. — Ivanov & Lenkey, *Do leveraged ETFs really amplify late-day returns and volatility?*; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2018.09.001
C4 [CONFIRMED] (T2, Rev. Finance 2016, sample Jun 2006–Jul 2011, 52 LETFs / 346 large-cap names): End-of-day realized vol co-moves with rebal/volume ratio; strongest in **15:45–16:00 / 16:00–16:15** windows. Front-run strategy (buy 2x/−2x SPX LETF ~14:30 on top-decile rebal days, exit close) ≈ **+66 bps/day gross** — **not significantly better than pure ±2% momentum by 14:45 (~60 bps)**; look-ahead on top-decile; **no net-cost edge established**. — Shum, Hejazi, Haryanto & Rodier, *Intraday Share Price Volatility and Leveraged ETF Rebalancing*; https://doi.org/10.1093/rof/rfv061

Selective/crisis sample + simultaneity (rebal uses full-day r including late window) → upward bias (Lenkey). — Bai, Bond & Hatch; https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-6229.12061
C6 [CONFIRMED] (T2 WP/SSRN 2021, sample Jan 2012–Dec 2019, optionable stocks in ≥1 equity LETF basket): Both option γ-hedge and LETF rebal explain **last-30-min** stock returns; **1 SD LETF rebal flow → +430% of average last-half-hour return** (units: multiple of mean \(r_\mathrm{end}\), not bps; **NO-COST-MODEL** for a trade). LETF channel **declines over sample** (barely significant late); γ persists. LETF pressure only in **final** window (swap MMs “bound to close”); example: AAPL 2018-10-24 rebal ≈ **8.85% of last-half-hour ADV**. Scales rebal by last-half-hour ADV: \(\Omega^\mathrm{LETF}_{j,t}=\sum_i L_i(L_i-1)A_{i,t-1}w_{i,j}r^\mathrm{pre}_{i,t}/\mathrm{ADV}^\mathrm{end}_{j}\). — Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi & Moerke, *Liquidity Provision to Leveraged ETFs and Equity Options Rebalancing Flows*; https://abarbon.com/assets/Liquidity_Provision_to_Rebalancing_Flows_from_Leveraged_ETFs_and_Equity_Options.pdf / SSRN 3925725

Front-run **+402% ann. gross / −59% net of spread**. Cheng formula is stated to apply to single-stock products, but impact OOS for the single-stock-ETF era is **UNKNOWN**. Practitioner T3 claims of large daily rebal notional are not peer-reviewed economics.

### Economics sketch
- **Expected gross (cited, units explicit):**
  - Average late-day **return impact** after flow adjust (Ivanov–Lenkey): **≪ 1 bps** on typical stock-days (fraction of <50 bps late-day moves).
  - Conditional on large common move / early literature (Tuzun): **~6–13 bps** late-day return for 1% index day (overstated methodology).
  - Brøgger VIX: **~6 bps** average late-day price impact.
  - Barbon: 1 SD LETF flow = **4.3× mean last-30-min return** (not a bps figure; mean \(r_\mathrm{end}\) in sample is near 0 with sd ~36 bps — impact is a regression multiple, **NO-COST-MODEL**).
  - Shum front-run: **~60–66 bps/day gross** on selected days — matches plain momentum, **not established net**.
  - Cheng MOC share: **~17% of median MOC** at 1% (overstated).
- **Our cost burden:** Auction exit ≈ 0 exit spread; entry taker ~1–3 bps on mega-caps (champion structure). Continuous mid-afternoon entry + exit at close faces full half-spread×2 and adverse selection.
- **Net prior:** Field prior on **average** liquid-name days is **near zero net** after costs. Edge, if any for us, is **conditional** (high predicted |flow| days × name with large single-stock LETF complex) and/or **as a NOII/basis conditioner**, not unconditional late-day momentum. Compare: champion = **+2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 holdout**.
- **Comparison line:** champion = **+2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 holdout**.

- **Hypothesis:** Predicted LETF close-flow intensity \(F_{i,t}=\sum_j (L_j^2-L_j)\,A_{j,t-1}\,w_{j,i}\,r^{(j)}_{t}\) (single-stock: \(w=1\); index: QQQ/SOXX/SPY weights) ranks names NVDA ≥ TSLA ≫ AVGO/NFLX/LRCX over 2024–2026 and co-moves with **signed NOII / late-day return / auction basis** in the same order; high-|F| days raise champion-style |basis| fire rate or signed auction drift **in the direction of F**.
- **Named payer:** LETF daily rebal (swap-counterparty hedge into underlying/futures), price-insensitive, end-of-day.
- **Data:** Owned 1s bars + Databento NOII; free/≤$100 daily AUM or shares-outstanding history 2022–2026 (issuer sites, SEC N-PORT quarterly degraded OK). **No new real-time feed.**

- **A-priori thresholds:** Pre-register F construction (prior-day AUM, day r through 15:30 or 15:50); rank correlation of mean |F| vs mean late-day pressure metrics; optional interact with champion |basis|≥10 bps @ 15:55:10.
- **Promotion:** Rank match NVDA/TSLA top + signed co-movement of F with auction imbalance/return significant OOS (never-fit names or forward M10 window) with economic ≥+2 bps/event net on high-F ∩ basis events.
- **Kill:** Rank mismatch (AVGO/NFLX/LRCX ≥ NVDA in realized close pressure despite lower F); or F adds no incremental explanatory power beyond plain day-return momentum; or net ≤0 after 2 bps entry on n≥250.

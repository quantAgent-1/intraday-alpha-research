## DR-Q7 — A academic findings
### Verdict recommendation
**OPEN-TESTABLE** (confidence **MED**) — Peer-reviewed NYSE/Nasdaq closing-auction literature (2010–2021 samples) supports that the **same passive/index MOC payer** that funds the Nasdaq champion also drives NYSE closes, and that **NYSE exhibits more temporary price pressure than Nasdaq**: auction mid-to-close deviations ~**+1.2 bps larger** on NYSE, overnight reversals **~2×** larger, and last-minute floor-broker D-Orders systematically corrupt pre-3:55 indicative accuracy (near-to-close error jumps ~**40 bps** when D-Orders enter the public feed at 3:55). Imbalance-sorted strategies at first dissemination are **significantly profitable** on both venues (Jegadeesh & Wu JFE 2022; gross, NO-COST-MODEL at retail scale). No T2 paper runs the **exact champion rule** (15:55:10 near−mid |basis|≥10 bps → take WITH basis → exit 16:00 cross) on NYSE-only liquid names, and samples end before the **2024** NYSE dissemination/cutoff amendments — so port is **plausible, not confirmed**. Effect-size prior: unconditional large-cap abs. auction deviation **~2.7 bps** (same order as champion **+2.5 bps/event** *selected*); filtered signed edge UNKNOWN.
What would flip it: NYSE-only replication of champion economics with n≥250 events delivering ≥+2.5 bps/event gross (dev-quality) or holdout-comparable means on post-2020 (ideally post-2024-rule) history; *or* a clean null (≤0 net) after powering, which would support **EXHAUSTED-BY-FIELD** for the basis port despite stronger overnight reverse.
### Mechanism
**Who pays:** Price-insensitive **index mutual funds, passive trackers, and ETF-related rebalancers** forced to the listing-venue close to minimize tracking error vs official closing prices used for NAV/benchmarks; option MMs unwind delta at the close on expiration Fridays; month-end/window-dressing and index reconstitution days spike auction volume. Same payer family as Nasdaq champion (Bogousslavsky & Muravyev JFM 2023 DiD: Auction×Passive / Auction×ETF elasticities strongly positive; active MF ownership not).
**Why temporary / why NYSE differs:** Auction absorbs one-sided MOC; continuous mid at 4pm is not the clearing price. NYSE hybrid design gives floor brokers **late exclusive D-Order** window (enter/modify to 3:59:50; historically excluded from public imbalance stats until 3:55), so retail-visible near/imbalance is incomplete and can **flip** late (~$3.1M / ~13% of auction volume average sign-change size in Hu-Murphy). Result: more last-minute abnormal imbalances and **stronger overnight reverse** than all-electronic Nasdaq. COVID floor closure → pre-3:55 indicative quality converged toward Nasdaq (causal support for D-Order channel).
**Persistence / capacity:** Passive AUM growth structural; listing-venue monopoly on the close persists. Capacity at $1k–$10k is unconstrained by impact (large-cap auction impact for tiny ADV fractions is sub-bps). **Adversarial risk for retail basis trade:** floor brokers / D-Order clients see better late information and can move the close after a 15:55 retail decision — argues for a **later** decision instant (post D-Order inclusion) or smaller expected hit-rate than Nasdaq champion at identical clock time.
**Coherent payer found:** yes — indexed MOC into NYSE-listed names; mechanism is the same family as champion, with an additional NYSE-specific friction (D-Orders) that may *amplify* temporary dislocation but *degrade* early indicative quality.
### Claims
C1 [CONFIRMED] (T2, JFM 2023 / WP June 2021, sample 2010–2018 NYSE+Nasdaq common stocks p>$5, mcap>$100M): Closing auction share of dollar volume rose **3.1%→7.5%**; mean abs. auction price deviation from 4pm mid = **8.1 bps** (large-cap quintile **~2.66 bps**); matches pre-close bid or ask in **68.5%** of cases; deviations reverse **almost fully overnight** (~85% path; 1/3–1/2 within 30 min after-hours when liquid) — noise/price pressure, not information. ETF and **passive** (not active) ownership strongly raise auction turnover. — Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, *Who Trades at the Close?*, J. Financial Markets 66:100852; PDF https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf
C2 [CONFIRMED] (T2, same paper): NYSE auction abs. deviations are **~1.2 bps larger** than Nasdaq (pre-auction similar); closing-price deviations also rise for firms that **switch Nasdaq→NYSE**. Imbalance dissemination contributes to pre-close price discovery (NYSE started earlier than Nasdaq in sample) but magnitude is small relative to residual auction noise. — Bogousslavsky & Muravyev 2021/2023, same URL.
C3 [CONFIRMED] (T2, JFE 2022, sample ~decade ending ~2019; peak auction ~**10% ADV**): NYSE closing auctions offer **more depth** than Nasdaq; temporary component of price impact dissipates over **3–5 days** on both; strategies that sort on **first-disseminated order imbalance** (long high / short low imbalance deciles, exit at close and multi-day reverse) are **significantly profitable** (gross; NO-COST-MODEL). Linear model avg impact ~**2.35 bps** per 1% ADV. — Jegadeesh & Wu, *Closing auctions: Nasdaq versus NYSE*, JFE 143(3):1120–1139; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.12.003
C4 [CONFIRMED] (T2, Management Science 2025 / WP 2020 “Vestigial Tails,” sample S&P 500 2011–2018 + COVID floor-closure natural experiment): NYSE indicative **near price** is far less accurate than Nasdaq until D-Orders enter the public feed at **3:55** (~**40 bps** mean abs near−close error reduction at that instant); overnight price reversals of auction dislocations are **~2× larger on NYSE** than Nasdaq; last-minute abnormal imbalances (esp. where floor-broker fees inhibit competition) explain stronger reversals; floor closure improved pre-3:55 paired volume 55%→85% and near accuracy **~100 bps**, then reversed on floor reopen. — Hu & Murphy, *Vestigial Tails? Floor Brokers at the Close…*, Mgmt Sci 2025; slides https://microstructure.exchange/slides/20201006%20Microstructure%20Exchange%20-%20WEB.pdf ; https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.00884
C5 [CONFIRMED] (T2, same Hu-Murphy / SSRN abstract): NYSE imbalance **sign changes** after late D-Order activity are sizable — average about **$3.1 million or 13.0% of closing auction volume** — so early public imbalance is an incomplete sufficient statistic for the final cross. D-Orders can add to *or flip* the published imbalance. — Hu & Murphy SSRN abstract_id=3600230.
C6 [CONFIRMED] (T2, JFQA 2026 / Goyal–Jegadeesh–Wu, sample Jan 2012–Dec 2021 NYSE+Nasdaq): Closing-auction price impact is **lower than continuous market** for all but Nasdaq microcaps; for large stocks, 0.5% ADV auction impact ~**3.2 bps (NYSE)** / **7.2 bps (Nasdaq)** under square-root model; 1% ADV mean impact ~17.7 bps (median 8.4) under sqrt vs JW linear 2.35 — model-sensitive. Auction is a cheap venue for small retail notional. — Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu, *Price Impact in Closing Auctions…*, JFQA 2026; https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109025102592
C7 [PLAUSIBLE] (T2 abstract/WP, Wu 2019 SSRN 3440239, sample ~2010–2018 proprietary NYSE closing data): Passive investing → greater MOC usage; closing-auction imbalance direction predicts contemporaneous price impact; effect coefficients larger on Nasdaq (0.276) than NYSE (0.185) in one reported specification — consistent with deeper NYSE auction liquidity absorbing flow with less price move. NO-COST-MODEL. — Wu, *Closing Auction, Passive Investing, and Stock Prices*.
C8 [CONFIRMED] (T2 classic, Cushing & Madhavan JFM 2000, sample Russell 1000 1997–1998): Published MOC order-imbalance indications produce systematic **return reversals** — temporary liquidity pressure. **DECAY-UNKNOWN** for 2020s passive-dominated regime; useful only as mechanism ancestry. — https://doi.org/10.1016/S1386-4181(99)00012-9
C9 [PLAUSIBLE] (T3 thesis, Morand Imperial MSc 2020): Linear models on imbalance predict last-10-min forward returns; claim ~**30% of spread** capture; **Nasdaq easier than NYSE**; on NYSE, short-horizon R² improves nearer the **3:59:50 D-Order cutoff**. Not peer-reviewed; NO-COST-MODEL; useful as hypothesis that NYSE signal quality peaks later than Nasdaq’s 15:55:10 champion snapshot. — https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/faculty-of-natural-sciences/department-of-mathematics/math-finance/MORAND_CLEA_01805978.pdf
C10 [UNVERIFIED] (gap, academic): No T2 source reports fire rate, mean bps/event, or hit-rate of a **|near−mid| ≥ 10 bps @ ~15:55** (or post-3:55) rule on **NYSE-listed only** liquid names with auction exit. 2024 NYSE rule changes (earlier/more frequent imbalance dissemination; D-Order share now ~46–60% of executed close) are **outside** all cited sample periods → **DECAY-UNKNOWN / regime-shift risk** for port of pre-2020 coefficients.

### Economics sketch
- **Expected gross (cited literature, not champion filter):** Large-cap abs. auction−mid deviation **~2.7 bps** (Bogousslavsky); NYSE **+1.2 bps** vs Nasdaq; overnight reverse **2×** Nasdaq (Hu-Murphy). JW imbalance long/short “significantly profitable” (no per-event bps published in freely read sources). Square-root auction impact for large NYSE ~**3–4.5 bps per 1% ADV** — irrelevant at our size. Unconditional means are **not** the filtered |basis|≥10 edge; champion’s **+2.5 / +12.5** is a *selected* event mean.
- **Our cost burden:** Auction exit = single print (0 exit spread); entry taker half-spread on liquid NYSE large-caps often **1–3 bps**; zero commission through 2026-12-31. Exchange auction fees sub-bps at retail share counts.
- **Net prior:** If champion-style |basis|≥10 bps fires at similar rate and D-Order noise does not destroy directional accuracy post-3:55, **gross in the +2 to +5 bps/event band is the academic prior** (comparable to champion dev, not holdout). If early near is garbage until D-Orders print, fire rate of *true* basis collapses or hit-rate falls → net ≤0. Overnight reverse literature is larger on NYSE but **out of session rules**.
- **Comparison line:** champion = **+2.5 bps/event dev / +12.5 holdout**.
### Proposed next test (only if OPEN-TESTABLE)
- **Hypothesis:** On liquid NYSE-listed names, a pre-registered |indicative near − continuous mid| ≥ 10 bps rule, direction WITH the near, entry taker, exit at official close print, delivers mean gross ≥ +2.5 bps/event on a **post-3:55** snapshot (D-Orders included) with hit-rate ≥55% and n≥250. Secondary: same rule at 15:55:10 (pre full D-Order inclusion) underperforms the post-3:55 snapshot — testing Hu-Murphy incompleteness claim.
- **Named payer:** Indexed/passive MOC into NYSE listing-venue close (same family as champion).
- **Data:** Databento XNYS (or NYSE historical order-imbalance product) near/imbalance + SIP mid for 10–30 liquid NYSE names, preferably 2020→2026 and stratified pre/post Aug-2024 rule change; budget target ≤$100 one-time (exact SKU/price = modality C). Owned: none of XNYS imbalance.
- **Universe:** Liquid NYSE common stocks (e.g. mega-cap industrials/financials/staples; exclude ADRs/preferreds); never-fit relative to Nasdaq champion names.
- **Expected n & power:** Daily × years × names → n>500 if fire rate similar to Nasdaq large-caps; if fire rate << or D-Order flips destroy signal, underpowered — report fire-rate and sign-stability of imbalance 15:55→16:00 as primary diagnostics. Noise ~20 bps/event → need large n for 2–3 bps mean.
- **A-priori thresholds:** Primary snapshot = first second **after** D-Orders are in the public feed (historically ~15:55:00; confirm T1 for 2026); |near−mid|/mid ≥ 10 bps; direction with near; exit auction print; no ML first pass. Secondary = 15:55:10 exact champion clock for apples-to-apples venue compare.
- **Promotion:** Pooled gross ≥ +2.5 bps/event and hit-rate ≥55% on pre-registered window with n≥250 and not concentrated in <5 sessions → stage-B forward paper only (holdout spent).
- **Kill:** Fire rate <1 event/name-week; or gross ≤0 net of 2 bps entry burden; or edge entirely in pre-2024 sample and null post-2024; or imbalance sign flip rate after decision instant >X% (pre-register X from Hu-Murphy ~13% volume-weighted baseline).
- **Trial family charged:** **NYSE closing-auction basis** (new family; do not pool with Nasdaq champion / M11).
